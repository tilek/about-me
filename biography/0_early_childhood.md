# My Birth and Early Childhood

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Why Was I Born?

When I asked my dad how many years he was together with my mom before they got  
married, he said that back then it was counted in months or even weeks.  

So for them, it was just several months, as was usual.  

They met in modern-day Bishkek, the capital of Kyrgyzstan. Back then and  
up until 1991 it was all still a part of the Soviet Union and the city  
used to be called Frunze after one of the commanders of Lenin's revolution.  

There, they studied at the main engineering university in the country.  
My dad studied construction engineering and my mom studied architecture.  

## Why Was I Named "Tilek"?

After getting married in 1980 my parents struggled to have their first child for  
several years. My understanding is that it was likely because my mother was too  
stressed living with her mother-in-law in her house.  

To address the issue my dad hustled his way into getting a studio apartment from  
the Soviet government. They moved and soon after I was born on June 9, 1985.  

To signify that I was a child they had long dreamed about my dad suggested calling  
me "Tilek" because in Kyrgyz it means "a wish" or "a dream".  

I feel deeply pleased, happy, and content knowing I was wanted and waited  
for so much! That's the main reason I love my name so much!  

## How Was I Born?

The actual birth turned out to be difficult for my mom. I was upside down in her  
belly and the doctor recommended a Cesarean section. She was under full  
anesthesia but she was told that the birth was very bloody and she had multiple  
long-term side effects afterward.  

My parents tell me that I often got sick and they spent a lot of time constantly  
bringing me to doctors and treating my illnesses.  

Another detail I found out only recently is that immediately after birth I was  
removed into a special room and felt my mother’s warmth only several days later.  

I sometimes wonder if it could have been the basis of anxiety running deep in  
my subconscious and the anxious attachment I constantly feel but which I hope  
to heal someday.  

## Who Were My Caregivers?

I was the first out of two children in a full family with both of my parents.  

For 4.5 years, I was the only child and had a lot of attention from my mother  
who stayed at home during that whole period.  

My grandma helped my parents care for me as well during the first 5 years or so  
until my little brother Meder and my little cousin Aidar were both born in 1990.  
She then switched to help her other daughter Kulipa care for Aidar.  

## Years 1 to 6

Overall, I think those times were mainly happy for me.  

I remember playing a lot outside alone and with other children in our apartment  
building block. I also often went to my mother's older brother Taalai. In Kyrgyz,  
"mother's brother" is called "tayake", so I always call him "Taalai tayake".  

There, I played with his two daughters (my older cousin sisters) Alima and Altynai.  
I even called them "Alima-Altynai" as a single unit which sounded funny for  
everyone.

## Main Early Childhood Traumas

Looking back at my childhood traumas as an adult, two parenting patterns stand out.  
Both of them were very popular in that region during that time period.  

1) Punitive parenting  

My mom had what I think is a popular style of parenting for Asia and USSR  
at that time. It means I was yelled at or sometimes physically beaten  
if I didn't behave in a "proper" way.  

I don't consciously remember any instance of physical punishment but I feel it 
in my body and my mom admits that there were some. There were definitely not  
bloody or in any similar violent but enough for me to be subconsciously scared  
of her for decades later.  

One of the earliest memories I have is when I pooped my pants while playing  
outside. "It was the exact moment when my consciousness went live" as one famous
comedian once joked talking about a very similar first memory.  

When that happened, I got very scared my mom would beat me. I was ashamed,  
uncomfortable  but afraid to go home. It's very sad to remember for sure.  

My dad did not spend as much time parenting as my mom and did not think very  
consciously about her parenting style either. He was focused on always providing  
enough for healthy food, high-quality clothing, good vacations at a lake resort,  
and generally good living for our middle-class / upper-middle-class urban family
during very challenging transition times from Soviet Union to newly gained  
independence and conversion to capitalism for Kyrgyzstan. 

His amount of care and attention was probably much more than usual for that  
time too but I always wished I had more.  

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2) Playing unsupervised in the streets  

Starting around age 4 to 6 I was already playing with other children in  
the courtyard shared between apartment blocks that is open to passers-by.  

First of all, it caused one incident where I almost fell from a roof of a  
several story building.  

Second of all, there was an incident related to the "pants area". 

It became a subconscious trauma that I remembered only accidentally and under
influence in my 30s. It was very difficult to realize and took many months  
of psychotherapy to process and integrate.  

And no, it wasn't as bad as being touched inappropriately but it was very    
traumatic regardless. And I am sure it wouldn't have happened if I was  
supervised at the level that is typical even in Kyrgyzstan nowadays. So, I  
thought for historic reasons it is worth making it very obscurely public.  

## What Helped Me The Most During Those Times?

In addition to all the care and attention I received from 3 of my caregivers,  
I already felt being unique, special and admired.  

As long as I remember her, my grandma loved retelling everyone the story how I  
learned to read already at age of 4.  

She and my parents told me that whenever they took  
a bus, I loved reading aloud all the signs I could see. Based on such wealth  
compliments from my grandmother I already felt I was a genius and I was special.  

I am infinitely grateful to her for feeling so much love, care, attention,  
and admiration from her side. It definitely helped build a strong foundation  
for my self-belief and ambition during my whole life after.
