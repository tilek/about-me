# My Birth and Early Childhood

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Why Was I Born?

When I asked my dad how many years he was together with my mom before they got 
married, he said back then it was counted in months or even weeks. 😂 
So, with them it was just several months as was usual.  

They met in modern-day Bishkek, the capital of Kyrgyzstan. Back then and  
up until 1991 it was all still a part of Soviet Union and the city  
used to be called Frunze after one of the commanders during Lenin's revolution.  

There they studied in the main engineering university in the country.  
My dad studied construction engineering and my mom studied architecture.  

## Why I Was Named "Tilek"?

After getting married in 1980 my parents struggled to have their first child for  
several years. My understanding is that it was likely because my mother was too  
stressed living with her mother-in-law at her house.  

To address the issue my dad hustled his way into getting a studio apartment from  
Soviet government. They moved and soon after I was born on June 9, 1985.  

To signify that I was a child they long dreamed about my dad suggested calling  
me "Tilek" as in Kyrgyz it means "a wish" or "a dream".  

I feel deeply pleased, happy, and content knowing I was wanted and waited  
for so much! That's the main reason I love my name so much too!  

## How Was I Born?

Actual birth turned out to be difficult for my mom. I was upside down in her  
belly and doctor recommended to do a Ceasar's section. She was under full  
anesthesia but she was told the birth was very bloody and she had multiple  
long-term side effects after.  

My parents tell me that I often got sick and they spent a lot of time constantly
bringing me to doctors and curing my diseases.  

Another detail I found out only recently, is that immediately after birth I was  
removed into a special room and felt my mother after birth only several  
days later.  

I can't know for sure but I strongly feel it could have been the basis of  
anxiety running deep in my subconscious and anxious attachment I constantly feel  
but hopefully some day will cure.  

## My Caregivers

I was the first out of two children in a full family with both of my parents.  

For 4.5 years, I was the only child and had a lot of attention from my mother
who stayed at home during that whole period.

My grandma helped my parents care for me as well during the first 5 years or so  
until my little brother Meder and my little cousin Aidar were both born in 1990.  
She then switched to help her other daughter Kulipa care for Aidar.  

## Years 1 to 6

Overall, I think those times were mainly happy for me.  

I remembering playing a lot outside alone and with other children in our apartment
building block. I also often went to my mother's older brother Taalai. In Kyrgyz,
"mother's brother" is called "tayake", so I always call him "Taalai tayake".

There I played with his two daughters (my older cousin sisters) Alima and Altynai.
I even called them "Alima-Altynai" as a single unit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
## Main Challenges Between Years 1 to 6

In terms of challenges, I think there were 4 main ones typical for Soviet Union  
of that era.  

1) My mom had what I think is a popular style of parenting for Asia and USSR  
at that time. It means I was yelled at or sometimes physically beaten  
if I didn't behave in a "proper" way.  

One of the earliest memories I have is when I pooped my pants while playing  
outside. I got so scared my mom would beat me, I was ashamed, uncomfortable  
but afraid to go home. It's very sad to remember.  

My dad did not spend as much time parenting as my mom and did not think very 
consciously about her parenting style and was mainly focused on earning a living

I think it was usual at a time too but still painful regardless.

2) Starting around age 4, 5, or 6 I was already playing unsupervised with other
children in the courtyard shared between apartment blocks that is open to anyone 
passing by.

This caused one incident where I almost fell from a roof of a several
story building and another incident related to the "pants area". It became a
subconscious trauma that I only remembered accidentally under influence in my 30s
and was difficult to realize and process.

And no, it wasn't as bad as being touched inappropriately but deeply traumatic 
regardless. And I am sure it wouldn't have happened if I was supervised at the  
level that is typical even in Kyrgyzstan nowadays.  

## What Helped Me The Most During Those Times?

At that time, I already felt special and admired.

As long as I remember her, my grandma loved retelling the story how I already 
learned to read when I was 4. She and my parents told me that whenever they took
a bus, I loved reading aloud all the signs I could see. Based on such wealth
compliments from my grandmother I already felt I was a genius and I was special.  

I am infinitely grateful to her for feeling so much love, care, attention,  
and admiration from her side. It definitely helped build a strong foundation  
for my self-belief and ambition during my whole life after.
